    Cuff Pressure Sensor

    Solution Pros Cons

    Option 1.
    Aratas (Omron) 2SMPP-03 MEMS Gauge Blood Pressure Sensor
    $8.36/each
    link to product    		 * Inexpensive ($8.36), keeping total subsystem cost well under the $60 course budget
    * Unamplified piezoresistive Wheatstone bridge output (0 to 42 mV) directly satisfies the EGR 304 Permitted Analog Sensor requirement for custom amplification and active filtering
    * Purpose-built for medical blood pressure cuffs (0 to 50 kPa / 0 to 375 mmHg gauge range) with a built-in tube port    		 * Requires a 3-op-amp instrumentation amplifier stage in addition to an active band-pass filter
    * Uses surface mounting instead of through-hole pins

    * Option 2.
    * STMicroelectronics MPX2050GP Unamplified Bridge Pressure Sensor
    * $23.87/each
    * Link to product    		 * Unamplified Wheatstone bridge (0 to 40 mV output) satisfies the EGR 304 requirement for a custom non-trivial amplifier and filter circuit
    * Through-hole 4-SIP package with a 4.93mm barbed port is easy to solder on a Peralta PCB
    * Covers the ideal 0 to 50 kPa (0 to 375 mmHg) vented gauge pressure range    		 * Nearly 3x more expensive than the Omron 2SMPP-03 ($23.87 vs. $8.36)
    * Requires 10V, so 5V rail reduces output to 20 mV

    * Option 3.
    * Amphenol Advanced Sensors (NovaSensor) NPC-120T
    * $8.18/each
    * Link to product    		 * Inexpensive ($8.18)
    * Very high maximum pressure (125PSI) minimizes chance of sensor breaking under unanticipated load
    * Robust voltage input (1-10V)    		 * Small SMD component may be difficult to solder on PCB
    * No built-in tube port will make attaching tubes difficult

    Table 1: Cuff Pressure Sensor component selection

    Choice: Option 3: Amphenol Advanced Sensors (NovaSensor) NPC-120T

    Rationale: Option 2 is much too expensive, and between Option 1 and Option 3, Option 3 is more robust while also being slightly cheaper.

    Operational Amplifier (Op Amp)

    Solution Pros Cons

    Option 1.
    Microchip MCP6004-I/P Quad Rail-to-Rail Op Amp (14-PDIP)
    $0.60/each
    link to product    		 * Inexpensive ($0.60) and also available on hand
    * Houses 4 rail-to-rail op-amps in a single through-hole 14-PDIP package, allowing a 3-op-amp instrumentation amplifier and a 4th-stage active band-pass filter on one IC
    * Swings across the full 0V to 5V range on our single 5V regulated rail with 1 MHz gain-bandwidth product
    * Already proven to work    		 * 6.0V absolute maximum supply rating means it must strictly be powered from the regulated 5V rail, never the 9V rail
    * 14-pin DIP footprint takes slightly more PCB space than an 8-pin DIP

    * Option 2.
    * Microchip MCP6002-I/P Dual Rail-to-Rail Op Amp (8-PDIP)
    * $0.45/each
    * Link to product    		 * Compact 8-pin PDIP through-hole package is very easy to breadboard and solder
    * Full 0V to 5V rail-to-rail input and output swing maximizes PIC18F57Q43 ADC resolution
    * Ultra-low 100 uA quiescent current per channel    		 * Only contains 2 op-amp channels, requiring two separate chips if building a 3-op-amp differential instrumentation amplifier plus an active filter stage
    * Maximum supply voltage is limited to 6.0V

    * Option 3.
    * Texas Instruments LM358P Dual General-Purpose Op Amp (8-PDIP)
    * $0.33/each
    * Link to product    		 * Wide 3V to 30V supply voltage range tolerates either the 5V or 9V power rail without damage
    * Very inexpensive ($0.33) in a standard 8-PDIP through-hole package
    * Low 500 uA supply current    		 * Not rail-to-rail output: saturates at roughly 3.5V when powered from a 5V rail, losing 30% of the PIC18F57Q43 ADC dynamic range
    * Contains only 2 channels and exhibits higher input bias current (20 nA vs. 1 pA CMOS)

    Table 2: Operational Amplifier component selection

    Choice: Option 1: Microchip MCP6004-I/P Quad Rail-to-Rail Op Amp (14-PDIP)

    Rationale: Option 1 is already familiar and known to function, and the additional Op Amps in the package allow for additional signal processing if needed.

    Character LCD Display

    Solution Pros Cons

    Option 1.
    Lumex LCM-S01602DTR/M 16x2 Character LCD Module (5V Parallel)
    $7.44/each
    link to product    		 * Inexpensive ($7.44) bare LCD module that complies with the EGR 304 ban on commercial breakout/daughterboards
    * Uses a standard 5V parallel GPIO interface, avoiding I2C and SPI protocols that are not covered in EGR 304
    * Transflective yellow-green LED backlight is easy to read under bright classroom lighting and draws only ~15-25 mA    		 * Requires 6 digital GPIO pins on the PIC18F57Q43 in 4-bit parallel mode
    * Requires an external 10k Ohm contrast trimmer potentiometer on the PCB

    * Option 2.
    * Newhaven Display NHD-0216K1Z-FL-YBW 16x2 Character LCD
    * $7.83/each
    * Link to product    		 * Direct 5V GPIO parallel interface (ST7066U controller) with complete mechanical and timing datasheets on DigiKey
    * High-contrast STN transflective display with yellow-green backlight
    * Standard 80mm x 36mm footprint fits within the 100mm x 100mm PCB limit    		 * Slightly more expensive ($7.83 vs. $7.44) for identical 16x2 character functionality
    * Also requires 6 GPIO pins and an external contrast resistor divider or potentiometer

    * Option 3.
    * Newhaven Display NHD-0216K1Z-NSW-BBW-L 16x2 White-on-Blue LCD
    * $7.44/each
    * Link to product    		 * Same low $7.44 price point and 5V parallel GPIO compatibility
    * Crisp white text on a blue background provides modern medical aesthetics
    * No daughterboard or serial backpack required    		 * Transmissive mode requires the LED backlight to be powered at all times in order to read the characters
    * Uses 6 digital I/O pins on the microcontroller

    Table 3: Display component selection

    Choice: Option 2: Newhaven Display NHD-0216K1Z-FL-YBW 16x2 Character LCD

    Rationale: Option 1 is out of stock, and between Option 2 and Option 3, Option 2 is transflective, meaning it is more visible in higher light conditions.

    H-Bridge Actuator Driver

    Solution Pros Cons

    Option 1.
    Fairchild Semiconductor FAN8100N Dual H-Bridge (12-DIP with heat pads)
    $1.16/each
    link to product    		 * Directly satisfies the EGR 304 Permitted Actuator requirement for a complex motor driver IC in the L293D family
    * 1.5A max current output
    * Already proven to work    		 * Bipolar Darlington outputs drop ~1.4V to 2.0V internally (though this safely steps our 9V rail down toward the 6V actuator rating)
    * Draws ~35 mA quiescent current on the logic and load rails

    * Option 2.
    * STMicroelectronics L293D Push-Pull Four-Channel Driver IC (16-PDIP)
    * $6.61/each
    * Link to product    		 * Explicitly named in the EGR 304 syllabus as an approved complex motor driver IC
    * Can be requested as a free sample directly from STMicroelectronics    		 * Lower continuous output current rating (600 mA per channel), leaving less thermal margin for a 500 mA air pump
    * Higher unit retail price ($6.61 vs. $4.39)

    * Option 3.
    * Custom Dual Low-Side Switch using 2x Infineon IRLZ44NPBF Logic N-MOSFETs
    * \(3.08/each (\)6.16 for 2)
    * Link to product    		 * Very low 22 mOhm on-resistance in a rugged TO-220 through-hole package
    * True 5V logic-level gate drive from PIC18F57Q43 PWM pins
    * High 47A current rating never overheats under pump loads    		 * Does not use a complex motor driver IC as recommended in the Permitted Actuators guidelines
    * Requires external flyback diodes and gate resistors on the PCB, and passes the full 9V supply to 6V actuators if PWM is set to 100%

    Table 4: H-Bridge Motor Driver component selection

    Choice: Option 1: Fairchild Semiconductor FAN8100N Dual H-Bridge

    Rationale: Option 1 is the lowest cost and is already familiar and proven to work.

    DC Air Pump

    Solution Pros Cons

    Option 1.
    Adafruit 4699 (ZR370-02PM) 4.5V-6V DC Diaphragm Air Pump (2.5 LPM)
    $7.95/each
    link to product    		 * Purpose-built 370-size sphygmomanometer diaphragm pump delivering 2.5 L/min flow and >400 mmHg stall pressure
    * Easily overcomes a constant passive bleed orifice to inflate an adult cuff to 180 mmHg in ~10-15 seconds
    * Stocked on DigiKey with full mechanical and electrical specifications ($7.95)    		 * Rated for 4.5V-6V nominal, requiring PWM duty-cycle limiting (~75-80%) when driven from our 9V H-Bridge rail
    * Draws ~500 mA peak current during inflation

    * Option 2.
    * Adafruit 4700 Mini DC Air Pump and Vacuum Motor (0.5 LPM)
    * $6.95/each
    * Link to product    		 * Lower current draw (~300 mA) reduces load on the 9V power supply
    * Ultra-compact footprint and \(1.00 lower cost (\)6.95)
    * Includes barbed ports for silicone tubing    		 * Low 0.5 L/min flow rate is 5x slower than Option 1 and struggles to reach 180 mmHg when fighting a constant bleed orifice
    * Smaller motor wears out faster under high back-pressure

    * Option 3.
    * SparkFun ROB-10398 12V DC Vacuum and Air Pump
    * $26.85/each
    * Link to product    		 * High flow rate and 16 inHg (~406 mmHg) pressure capability
    * 12V motor rating cannot be over-volted by a 9V supply
    * Full datasheet available on DigiKey    		 * Over 3x more expensive ($26.85 vs. $7.95), which would push our BOM close to the $60 budget limit
    * Under-driven on a 9V supply (~7.2V after H-Bridge drop), operating below its rated speed

    Table 5: DC Air Pump component selection

    Choice: Option 1: Adafruit 4699 (ZR370-02PM) 4.5V-6V DC Diaphragm Air Pump

    Rationale: Option 1 is the cheapest option that still has the required flow rate.

    Solenoid Air Valve

    Solution Pros Cons

    Option 1.
    Adafruit 4663 (FA0520E) 6V 3-Port Solenoid Air Release Valve
    $2.95/each
    link to product    		 * Inexpensive ($2.95) miniature 3-port medical cuff valve with full specifications on DigiKey
    * Fail-safe Normally Open (NO) exhaust path automatically vents cuff pressure if power is lost or measurement completes
    * Zero minimum pressure differential required to actuate from 0 to 350 mmHg    		 * 6V coil rating (~300-350 mA) requires ~75% PWM duty cycle when powered from our 9V H-Bridge rail
    * Plastic barbed ports require gentle handling during assembly

    * Option 2.
    * DFRobot DFR0866 6V 2-Position 3-Way Air Solenoid Valve
    * $4.00/each
    * Link to product    		 * Identical 3-port pneumatic switching behavior suitable for cuff exhaust control
    * Low coil current (~220-350 mA at 6V DC)
    * Stocked directly by DigiKey    		 * Costs 35% more ($4.00 vs. $2.95) than Option 1 for the same form factor and coil rating
    * Shorter wire leads require extension to reach the PCB terminal block

    * Option 3.
    * SparkFun ROB-10456 12V 2-Way Normally Closed Solenoid Valve
    * $9.62/each
    * Link to product    		 * Rugged valve body with standard 0.25-inch quick-disconnect spade terminals
    * Rated up to 116 PSI maximum pressure
    * Cannot be over-volted by a 9V rail    		 * Unsafe Normally Closed (NC) configuration traps pressure in the cuff if power fails
    * Pilot-operated liquid diaphragm requires at least 2.9 PSI (150 mmHg) pressure differential just to open and uses bulky 3/4-inch NPT pipe threads

    Table 6: Solenoid Air Valve component selection

    Choice: Option 1: Adafruit 4663 (FA0520E) 6V 3-Port Solenoid Air Release Valve

    Rationale: Option 1 is the cheapest option that is also normally open (NO).

    5V 1.5A Linear Voltage Regulator

    Solution Pros Cons

    Option 1.
    STMicroelectronics L7805CV 5V 1.5A Fixed Linear Voltage Regulator
    $0.55/each
    link to product    		 * Inexpensive ($0.55) and satisfies the EGR 304 requirement for a linear voltage regulator
    * Fixed 5.0V output at up to 1.5A requires only two decoupling capacitors and eliminates the risk of feedback resistor errors over-volting 5V ICs
    * Through-hole TO-220-3 package with internal thermal overload and short-circuit protection    		 * ~2.0V dropout voltage requires an input supply of at least 7.0V (easily met by our 9V wall adapter)
    * Dissipates (9V - 5V) * I_load as heat, though our 5V rail draws ~107 mA (~0.43W heat, no heatsink needed)

    * Option 2.
    * Texas Instruments LM2940CT-5.0/NOPB 5V 1.0A LDO Linear Regulator
    * $1.09/each
    * Link to product    		 * Low dropout voltage (0.5V typical) maintains a stable 5V rail even if the input sags to 6.0V
    * Includes reverse-battery protection in a standard TO-220-3 package
    * Easily handles our ~134 mA (with 25% margin) 5V rail load    		 * Lower maximum output current rating (1.0A vs. 1.5A on the L7805CV) and currently backordered on DigiKey
    * Requires a >=22 uF output capacitor within a strict ESR window (0.1 to 5 Ohm) to prevent oscillation

    * Option 3.
    * Texas Instruments LM317T/NOPB 1.25V-37V 1.5A Adjustable Linear Regulator
    * $1.29/each
    * Link to product    		 * Matches the 1.5A output current capability in a through-hole TO-220-3 package
    * Output voltage can be tuned with two external resistors
    * Built-in current limiting and thermal overload protection    		 * Requires two external precision resistors to set 5.0V; a cold solder joint on the ADJ pin could cause the output to jump toward 9V and destroy the PIC18F57Q43 and MCP6004
    * Costs more than twice as much as the fixed L7805CV ($1.29 vs. $0.55)

    Table 7: 5V Linear Voltage Regulator component selection

    Choice: Option 1: STMicroelectronics L7805CV 5V 1.5A Fixed Linear Voltage Regulator

    Rationale: Option 1 is the cheapest and meets all requirements.

    9V External Power Supply

    Solution Pros Cons

    Option 1.
    Bel Power Solutions SMI18-9-V-P5 9V 2.0A (18W) AC/DC Wall Adapter
    $23.11/each
    link to product    		 * Supplies up to 2,000 mA at 9V DC, which is more than enough
    * Standard 2.1mm x 5.5mm center-positive barrel plug connects directly to a PCB barrel jack
    * Complete UL/CE safety certifications and DigiKey datasheet    		 * Costs $23.11
    * Requires connection to a 120V AC wall outlet

    * Option 2.
    * SparkFun TOL-15314 9V 650mA AC/DC Wall Adapter
    * $9.25/each
    * Link to product    		 * Affordable ($9.25) with a standard 2.1mm x 5.5mm center-positive barrel plug
    * Lightweight and compact wall plug
    * Documented specifications on DigiKey and SparkFun    		 * Fails the Power Budget requirement
    * Insufficient current headroom for motor startup spikes

    * Option 3.
    * MPD BH26AASF 6xAA Alkaline Battery Holder (9V Nominal)
    * $1.23/each (plus 6x AA cells)
    * Link to product    		 * Very inexpensive hardware ($1.23) allowing untethered portable operation
    * Provides ~2,500 mAh capacity with 6 fresh 1.5V AA alkaline batteries
    * Includes datasheet and mechanical drawing on DigiKey    		 * Voltage sags below the 7.0V minimum dropout threshold of the L7805CV regulator as AA cells discharge under a ~1A pump/solenoid load
    * Requires batteries to be replaced frequently

    Table 8: 9V External Power Supply component selection

    Choice: Option 1: Bel Power Solutions SMI18-9-V-P5 9V 2.0A (18W) AC/DC Wall Adapter

    Rationale: Option 1 provides enough power to have a safety margin.

    Pin Assignments

    These are all the pins assigned on my microcontroller for all of the above components as well as debugging and communication components.

    Pin Subsystem MCC setting Connects to
    RD1 Buttons Digital input SW1 push button, 10k hardware pull-down (R13) with RC debounce (C6), active high
    RD2 Debug Digital output, start low D1 debug LED (Blue), 330Ω current-limiting resistor (R12), active high
    RA0 Pressure Sensor Analog input (ADC) Amplified_Pressure (Instrumentation Op-Amp U5C DC output)
    RA1 Pressure Sensor Analog input (ADC) Pressure_Signal (Band-pass filter Op-Amp U5D AC output)
    RB0 Pump Control Digital output, start low Pump_In (FAN8100N U3 Pin 11 FORWARDA), active high
    RB3 Valve Control Digital output, start low Valve_In (FAN8100N U3 Pin 2 FORWARDB), active high
    RD6 LCD Digital output, start low LCD RS (Pin 4)
    RD5 LCD Digital output, start low LCD E (Pin 6)
    RB5 LCD Digital output, start low LCD DB4 (Pin 11)
    RF7 LCD Digital output, start low LCD DB5 (Pin 12)
    RF6 LCD Digital output, start low LCD DB6 (Pin 13)
    RF5 LCD Digital output, start low LCD DB7 (Pin 14)
    RA2 External Comm Analog input / Analog output J2 External Board Connector (Pin 6)
    RA5 External Comm Analog input / Analog output J2 External Board Connector (Pin 7)
    GND Ground Board Earth net (Pins 15, 24, 33, 42, 52, and J2 Pin 8)
